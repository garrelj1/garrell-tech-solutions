[Trellis](https://github.com/garrell-tech-solutions/trellis) is a single-user scheduling assistant I built for one reason: opening it and saving a thought should be immediate and take no decisions. You type into a box, it is stored, and you decide later what it was. Capture now, triage later. Having to decide what a note _is_ before I can write it down is the biggest friction I hit with the note-taking apps I used before, and everything else here is secondary to fixing it.

## What friction was I actually trying to fix?

A thought shows up while you are doing something else: a checkout line, a meeting, walking back to the car. If the app wants a notebook, list, project, or label before it will keep the thought, you are doing two jobs at once, and the classifying is the one that fails:

- It lands in a wrong bucket nobody opens again.
- Or it never gets written down.

So the capture path gets two rules:

- **A budget.** The decision log holds capture to **50 ms**, asserted by the QA suite against a real server on a quiet machine, never in a unit or acceptance test ([`T-latency-is-a-qa-assertion`](https://github.com/garrell-tech-solutions/trellis/blob/trunk/docs/decisions.md)).
- **No browser in the way.** Trellis installs as a web app: home screen icon, its own window, no address bar, its own entry in the app switcher. That shipped as [issue #112](https://github.com/garrell-tech-solutions/trellis/issues/112) on 2026-08-24.

Cutting it left the page with no feedback at all: you tap `Pool` and the row just vanishes. The restyled row is the answer to that, and both halves shipped in the same slice.

## What does "capture now, triage later" mean in practice?

A capture is stored as soon as you submit it, and nothing classifies it. Triage is manual by decision until a language-model implementation lands much later ([`D-manual-triage-until-llm`](https://github.com/garrell-tech-solutions/trellis/blob/trunk/docs/decisions.md), settled 2026-08-17). No pre-filled guess to accept without reading.

When you do triage, it goes to one of three places:

- **Pool**, the default: errands, grouped by where you would do them.
- **Committed**, the exception, because a calendar you trust is one where a block exists only because it had to.
- **A quota**, for recurring hours. Filing into one creates the quota itself rather than parking a quota-flavored task in the task table, which is what [issue #138](https://github.com/garrell-tech-solutions/trellis/issues/138) closed on 2026-08-26.

Captures also carry a free-text context tag, autocompleting on prior values ([`D-context-tags-are-the-taxonomy`](https://github.com/garrell-tech-solutions/trellis/blob/trunk/docs/decisions.md)):

- Tags are the only taxonomy: numerous, cheap, disposable.
- They replaced a life-area model that asked what part of my life a task belonged to, a reporting question.
- The win I named in the log was batching by location, not finding a time slot: "much of my pool is errands attached to places."
- They shipped early because a tag cannot be backfilled.

## Which approach to quick capture fits which problem?

How I would characterize the options:

| Approach                       | Decision at capture | Decision later   | Risk it carries                                           |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------- | ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| File on the way in             | Where does this go? | None, in theory  | Filing competes with the thought, so capture is skipped   |
| One flat inbox, no triage step | None                | None             | Nothing consumes the inbox and it becomes a graveyard     |
| Auto-schedule everything       | None                | The tool decides | Blocks you did not choose lose the calendar's credibility |
| Capture, then triage           | None                | Kind, tag, date  | You have to actually come back and triage                 |

That last risk is what this design accepts in exchange for the other three.

## What happens after triage?

Three more screens, in a bottom tab bar alongside `Capture` ([`D-four-screens`](https://github.com/garrell-tech-solutions/trellis/blob/trunk/docs/decisions.md)):

- **Pool** groups errands by tag. Three or more open items on one tag form a _trip_, ordered by size, with no priority control to set. Fewer than three fall into loose ends. Once formed, a trip stays one until nothing is left open on it, so checking things off while you are out does not dismantle the list you are working from.
- **Committed** holds things with a time on them. Every item is either an _at_ (a fixed block, `WED 14:00`) or a _by_ (a deadline with slack in front of it, `BY 5 SEP`). They display differently and behave differently.
- **Quota** tracks recurring hours against a weekly target. Logging is retrospective: `+30m`, `+1h`, or an arbitrary duration, no start/stop timer. Counters reset Monday and shortfalls never carry forward ([`D-logging-is-retrospective-and-separate`](https://github.com/garrell-tech-solutions/trellis/blob/trunk/docs/decisions.md)), so a missed week is missed rather than turning into debt ([`D-quota-no-rollover`](https://github.com/garrell-tech-solutions/trellis/blob/trunk/docs/decisions.md)).

## What did dogfooding change that planning did not?

More than any review did. On 2026-08-20, after 187 commits and still no daily use, I re-planned the whole project around using it every day:

- **The target.** Daily use by 2026-09-03.
- **The reason,** from the log: "Capture works; nothing consumes what I capture. Every open milestone builds machinery for a plan I can't yet see."
- **The state.** Six pages, four of them read-only instruments feeding a solver that did not exist.
- **The new bar** for building that solver: two weeks of real use, plus my being able to name the friction automatic placement would remove. Or shorter: "If I can't name it, we don't build it."

Then the evidence beat the plan. In the two days after Trellis became reachable on a phone (over a tailnet; I have written separately about [using Tailscale to work from my phone](/blog/tailscale-swarmforge-on-the-go)), real use turned up four defects that every gate and every review had passed:

- [#101](https://github.com/garrell-tech-solutions/trellis/issues/101), scroll dead on touch, present in three shipped slices and flagged in all three pull request bodies without being fixed
- [#110](https://github.com/garrell-tech-solutions/trellis/issues/110), committing a task meant typing a UTC timestamp by hand
- [#119](https://github.com/garrell-tech-solutions/trellis/issues/119), every triage disclosure opening at once
- [#122](https://github.com/garrell-tech-solutions/trellis/issues/122), working a trip destroyed it

That last one is my favorite. You were in Home Depot with five items tagged `@homedepot`. You checked off the third, the group dropped below the trip threshold, the panel dissolved, and the last two scattered into loose ends, mid-shop. The screen destroyed the list at the moment it was being used for its only purpose. No test could see that, because the test does not go shopping. It closed on 2026-08-24, and trips have persisted through being worked since the slice that landed the next day.

So daily use became the standing rule for what gets built next ([`D-dogfooding-drives-the-roadmap`](https://github.com/garrell-tech-solutions/trellis/blob/trunk/docs/decisions.md), 2026-08-23). It pairs with an older rule there: every slice ends in something I can start the app and watch happen, recorded as my professional standard with clients rather than a preference about this project.

That is the same delivery discipline behind the [system-design and platform work I do at Garrell Tech Solutions](https://garrellts.com), and a relative of the [one-owner-per-rule architecture](/blog/one-owner-per-rule-architecture) approach: make the standard specific enough that nobody has to argue about it.

## What is Trellis built on?

Rust and SQLite, and not much else.

- **Core.** Pure interval arithmetic over sum types. No I/O, no `tokio`, enforced in CI, which is what keeps it property-testable.
- **Storage.** One SQLite file in WAL mode through `sqlx`.
- **Artifact.** A static musl binary plus that file.
- **Releases.** Every push to trunk publishes one tagged by commit date and run number, currently `v2026.08.27.210`. No version is bumped by hand.
- **Tenancy.** Single tenant, deferred rather than refused: "Not a never; right now I want single tenancy because I'm the only tenant."
- **Next.** Offline-first in the browser, a SQLite database in OPFS the browser owns, with optional sign-in to reach it from another device. Chosen 2026-08-25. A direction, not a schedule, and not designed yet.

Public under MIT, and listed on [my projects page](/projects).

## FAQ

**Can I run Trellis myself?**
Yes. `cargo build`, then `cargo run -p trellis-server -- serve --db trellis.db`, then open `http://localhost:8080`. Migrations run on startup, so a database path that does not exist yet is created and migrated.

**Does Trellis schedule my tasks for me?**
Not today. The `scheduler-core` crate is in the tree, but the milestone that would put it to work is paused. Automatically promoting an aging pool task onto the calendar is explicitly rejected: a block you did not choose is a block you learn to ignore.

**Does anything classify a capture automatically?**
No, by decision, until a language-model implementation lands at a much later milestone. A wrong pre-fill gets accepted silently; a blank field gets filled deliberately.

**Does it work offline?**
Not yet. It installs like a native app, but the data is entirely server-side, so caching the shell would wrap an empty screen in a frame. A service worker was ruled out at the time, deliberately. Offline is the current direction, unscheduled.

**Why a decision log instead of just issues?**
Because the expensive thing is re-deriving a call you already made. `docs/decisions.md` is append-only and records settled decisions with rejected alternatives and why, so an argument that already lost does not come back for free.
